# Brief 3.C: make the puppets look like the mocks

**Goal.** The art kit works, but the puppets do not look like the design: the rig points the sword straight along the line from base to tip, the body vanishes behind the ledge when the pointer is near, and each part's own outline and shadow show at every overlap. Rework the rig's pose model and the puppet view so the puppets on the dev page match mocks 5 and 6 and stay good-looking across the whole play area.

**Read first:** `CLAUDE.md`, `docs/DESIGN_SPEC.md` section 4, `docs/briefs/B-puppet-rig.md` and `docs/briefs/3.A-art-kit.md` (the original specs), `src/puppet/parts.ts`, `src/puppet/rig.ts`, `tests/unit/rig.test.ts`, `src/stage/art/puppetView.ts`, `src/stage/art/cutout.ts`, `dev/artkit.ts`. Study the puppets in `design/mocks/5-duel-attacking.html`, `6-duel-defending.html`, `1-title.html` and their screenshots: note the upright bowed neck rising from behind the ledge, the head on top, and the sword held near-horizontal toward the opponent or the target.

**Files you own:** `src/puppet/rig.ts`, `src/puppet/parts.ts` (scales only; do not move anchors, the presets depend on them), `tests/unit/rig.test.ts`, `src/stage/art/puppetView.ts`, `dev/artkit.ts`, `dev/artkit.html`, and `src/core/tunables.ts` + `tests/unit/tunables.test.ts` for the one change in item 4. Touch nothing else. Keep the exported signatures of `solveRig`, `RigPose`, `Placement`, `RigConfig`, `PuppetView` compatible (you may add optional fields); the workshop being built in parallel calls `solveRig` for its preview.

## Changes

1. **Pose model** (`solveRig`). The sword tip stays exactly at the pointer whenever it is reachable. Replace "everything in one line from the base" with:
   - A **rest point** for the head pin above the base: `rest = base + (-facing * restBack, -restHeight)` (new `RigConfig` fields; choose values so an idle puppet stands as in the mocks, roughly 190 to 230 px above its base).
   - The head and sword form one rigid line of length `R = headLength + swordLength` ending at the tip. The head pin `H` lies on the circle of radius `R` around the tip, at the point nearest the rest point: `H = tip + R * normalise(rest - tip)`; when `rest` and `tip` coincide use `(-facing, 0)`. So with the tip out in front at head height the sword is horizontal; when the tip is high or low the sword tilts; when the tip comes close the head leans back instead of the body disappearing.
   - Keep `H` sensible: never below the ledge line (`base.y - minHeadHeight`), never farther from the base than the body's maximum reach (then slide `H` along the circle or toward the tip so the reach limit holds; only if the tip is truly out of reach does `pose.tip` differ from the request).
   - The body is a smooth bowed curve from the base to `H`, leaving the base heading upward (so the neck rises from behind the ledge) and bowing away from the opponent. If the straight distance from base to `H` is shorter than `minSegments * segmentLength * minStretch`, the curve bows more to take up the slack rather than compressing or hiding segments. Segment count by curve length with the existing hysteresis.
   - The head's rotation follows the sword line, but limit how far the head can tip backward past vertical so it never turns upside down (clamp the sword line's angle to within about 100 degrees of facing-forward; if clamped, recompute `H` from the clamped direction).
   - Everything stays finite, deterministic, continuous in the tip for a fixed count, and exactly mirrored for the other facing.
2. **Scales.** Adjust `PART_SCALE` so sock segments are the mock's size (about 120 by 82 design px) and head and sword match mocks 5 and 6.
3. **Union outline** (`PuppetView`). In the mocks the white edge and the shadow wrap the whole puppet, not each part. Bake two textures per part: a **backing** (the hard shadow plus the white outline silhouette, no image) and a **face** (the image alone). In the container, put every part's backing below every part's face (two layers), so overlapping parts read as one cutout. Brass pins go on top. Still no per-frame filters. If `cutout.ts` lacks a way to bake the backing alone, do it inside `puppetView.ts` rather than editing `cutout.ts`.
4. **Target size.** The mocks draw targets at 44 px radius, which is 0.05 of the 880 px play width. Change the defaults in `src/core/tunables.ts` to `targetRadius: 0.05` and `targetRadiusTouch: 0.075` and update `tests/unit/tunables.test.ts` to match (this is a design-driven default change, not a weakened test). If any other test hard-codes 0.06 as the default and fails, report it instead of editing it.

## Tests (`tests/unit/rig.test.ts`)

Keep every existing invariant that still makes sense (finite values, tip reached, chain continuity, mouth meets handle, mirroring, hysteresis, continuity, out-of-reach clamp, stretch limits) and update tests that encoded the old straight-line model. Add: idle pose (tip level with the rest height, well in front) has a sword within 10 degrees of horizontal and the head pin within 40 px of the rest point's height; a tip close to the base (inside 150 px) still yields a visible body whose head pin is at least `minHeadHeight` above the base; the first segment leaves the base within 35 degrees of straight up; the head never rotates more than the clamp; for a dense grid of tips over the whole play area (x 200..1080, y 163..603) and both facings, the tip is reached exactly and no pin lies below the base.
